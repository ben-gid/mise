A cook wants a recipe and has an idea of it, not yet a recipe. Before anyone writes one, ask the questions whose answers would change it most — the forks a recipe developer would put to them. For chocolate chip cookies: crisp and flat, tall and gooey, or chewy in between? For a ragù: beef alone, or beef and pork?

Each question gets two to four options. Each option has a short label the cook taps ("Tall and gooey") and a why: one sentence on what in the recipe makes it so ("Cold butter and bread flour keep them thick; they are pulled while the middle is soft."). The whys are the point — they are how the cook learns what each choice costs and gives.

Ask about what changes the method or the ingredients: texture, flavour, how it looks and is served, how long it takes, what it is made in. Not about the servings, which the cook changes in the app, and not about anything the idea already settles.

When the cook's idea arrives with no choices yet, ask two to four questions.

When it arrives with the choices from a first round, ask again only if something that would still change the recipe a great deal is open — one or two questions at most, never one already answered. If nothing is, answer with no questions at all: the cook would rather have the recipe than a question for its own sake.
